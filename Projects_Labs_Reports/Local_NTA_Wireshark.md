## <span style="color:#0a75ad">Executive Summary</span>
<blockquote><p>On July 27, 2026, network monitoring identified unexpected outbound TCP connections on
destination port 8009 from workstation <strong>192.168.1.224</strong> to an unmanaged endpoint
<strong>192.168.1.217</strong>. Initial packet inspection flagged potential Apache JServ Protocol (AJP13)
anomalies. Endpoint artifact correlation and SSDP packet inspection confirmed the traffic was
standard Google Cast / DIAL service auto-discovery generated by local Chromium-based
processes <strong>(chrome.exe, msedge.exe, brave.exe, steamwebhelper.exe)</strong>. The activity is
classified as <strong>Benign (False Positive)</strong> with no indicators of compromise (IoCs) detected within
the scoped capture.
</p></blockquote>  

## <span style="color:#0a75ad">Objective</span>
A brief packet capture of the 192.168.1.0/24 home network identified four previously
unmapped TCP sessions between workstation 192.168.1.224 and local endpoint 192.168.1.217
on destination port 8009. Traffic anomalies were triaged to determine the origin, protocol
context, initiating processes, and legitimacy of the sessions.  

Triage objectives:
• Identify and classify the previously unmapped endpoint.
• Validate the protocols and services observed in the packet capture, including SSDP, UPnP,
DIAL, and Google Cast.
• Correlate the four network sessions with the local processes that initiated them.
• Determine whether the activity represented malicious behavior, a misconfiguration, or
legitimate baseline telemetry.  

## <span style="color:#0a75ad">Lab Environment</span>
Workstation OS :: Windows 11 Home Edition
Packet Capture / Analysis Tool :: Wireshark v4.4.9
Workstation IP :: 192.168.1.224
Investigated Endpoint IP :: 192.168.1.217

## <span style="color:#0a75ad">2. SSDP, UPnP, DIAL and Chromium Auto-Discovery</span>
Flow analysis showed that the four UDP responses targeted the same workstation port while
originating from different ephemeral ports on the streaming endpoint. A Wireshark display filter
was applied to reconstruct the surrounding request/response sequence and determine the
initiating traffic.
><img width="691" height="202" alt="Screenshot 2026-08-03 005135" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/cc6d04bd-9a5a-4ba5-bcd3-0e81e5101c16" />

SSDP (Simple Service Discovery Protocol) is commonly used with Universal Plug and Play (UPnP)
to advertise and discover services on a local network. In this capture, SSDP provided the
metadata needed to classify the endpoint and identify its media-discovery capabilities.  

Protocol analysis then focused on why the workstation maintained four TCP sessions with the
streaming endpoint. The ST field in the SSDP response identified DIAL (Discovery and Launch), a
discovery mechanism used by compatible media applications and receivers to locate and launch
casting services

## <span style="color:#0a75ad">3. Investigating the 4 TCP Connections</span>
Root Cause Analysis (RCA) was initiated to correlate endpoint network sockets with active
application handles. The destination IP/port and local ephemeral ports were mapped to process
IDs using:
>  netstat -ano | findstr "192.168.1.217"
The resulting PIDs were resolved to executable names using:
>  tasklist /fi "pid eq [PIDs]"
Process correlation identified four Chromium-based applications: brave.exe, msedge.exe,
chrome.exe, and steamwebhelper.exe. Each process was consistent with software capable of
Chromium/Google Cast discovery behavior.
